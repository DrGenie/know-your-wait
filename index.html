The evidence behind this tool

Why waiting times and costs matter, and the research study this tool comes from.

What the research says

  • Cost stops many people. In 2024-25, 1 in 4 people who needed a psychologist said cost was a reason they delayed or did not go (ABS 2025).
  • Waits are long and uneven. Under Better Access, the median wait from a GP plan to a first session was 22 days in 2021. People on the lowest incomes waited 22 days and those on the highest incomes 17 days (Pirkis et al. 2022).
  • Long waits mean missed appointments. People who wait longer are less likely to attend their first appointment (Loumidis and Shropshire 1997).
  • Support while waiting helps some people. Digital programs used while waiting were seen as useful, but many people stopped using them. Programs with a person guiding them kept people engaged better (Huang et al. 2024).
  • How numbers are shown matters. Showing chances as "X in 10" with simple pictures helps people with low numeracy understand them (Galesic et al. 2009).

The research study

This tool is part of the University of Newcastle pilot study Waiting, cost and certainty: preferences for access to psychological care. In it, 200 adults will choose between appointments with different waits and costs. Another 40 people will do the same tasks in an eye-tracking lab. The study will measure how much people value knowing their appointment date, and whether a picture or words is the better way to show waiting times. The results will be used to improve the waiting-list pages of this tool.

The study has not started. The numbers below are simulated to show what it will report.

    About this tool

    What it does

    Know Your Wait brings together free and low-cost mental health services in the Hunter, Medicare rules, and waiting-time information in one place. It does not book appointments or give medical advice.

    How it works

    • Find support checks each service against your answers. It never hides a service because you skipped a question. Services that do not fit are listed at the bottom with the reason.
    • Work out costs uses the Medicare benefit for a session of 50 minutes or more from 1 July 2026: $101.55 for a psychologist (MBS item 80110), $149.05 for a clinical psychologist and $89.50 for a mental health social worker or occupational therapist. Medicare covers up to 10 sessions a calendar year with a GP plan. Your health fund cannot top up a Medicare session, but it can pay for sessions Medicare does not cover.
    • My waiting lists treats a range such as "3 to 9 weeks" as an equal chance of being seen in each of those weeks. As time passes, it removes the weeks that have gone and works out the chance for the next 2 weeks.
    • For services uses the middle (median), 80th and 90th percentile of the waits you enter, rounded up to whole weeks.

    Privacy

    Everything runs in your web browser. What you enter is saved on this device only, so it is there when you come back. Nothing is sent to anyone.
