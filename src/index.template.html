    How it works

    Know Your Wait is built on research at the University of Newcastle. Two different kinds of information go into it, and they are never mixed.

    Choice study

    What matters to people

    Adults choose between appointments with different waits, costs and certainty.

    Used for: your best match and what matters to you

    Eye-tracking study

    How people read waiting times

    People read waiting times as words or as a picture while a camera follows their eyes.

    Used for: how waiting times are shown, and what we remind you to check

    Clinic records

    How long people really wait

    Clinics enter waits of people they have seen and of people still waiting.

    Used for: waiting times and your waiting-list tracker

    What you look at never changes your results

    If you turn on your camera, we show you where your eyes went and remind you what to double-check. Your best match comes only from your 6 choices.

    How sure are the numbers?

    • Your best match comes from your 6 choices and the choice study.
    • Chances of being seen are a rough guide. They come from what a clinic tells you, or from a clinic's records. They have not yet been checked against real appointment records. Always ask the clinic.
    • Costs are estimates unless the practice has told you its fee. Medicare amounts are from 1 July 2026.

    Your privacy

    • Everything runs in your web browser. Your answers are saved on this device only.
    • If you turn on your camera, the video stays on your device. It is never recorded, saved or sent. Nobody sees it. Only seconds spent on each part of the screen are kept, on this device.
    • When you share your plan, only the people you send it to can see it. You can use private wording with no health words.

    Why this matters

    • 1 in 4 people who needed a psychologist delayed or did not go because of cost (ABS 2025).
    • People on the lowest incomes wait longer for a first session than people on the highest incomes (Pirkis et al. 2022).
    • People who wait longer are less likely to go to their first appointment (Loumidis and Shropshire 1997).
    • Support while waiting helps more when a person guides it (Huang et al. 2024).
    • Showing chances as "X in 10" with simple pictures helps people understand them (Galesic et al. 2009).
    Technical details

    Study status. The study starts in 2027. All study figures in this prototype come from simulated data generated to match the study design. Hunter waiting times and fees for the example options are also simulated. Medicare rules, rebates and service details are real and were checked on 28 October 2026.

    How your 6 choices are used

    Prior for each weight (expected wait, weeks earlier or later, cost per session, video): the study estimate with a standard deviation of 50% of its size. We take 4,000 seeded draws, weight each by the conditional logit probability of the person's answers, and use the weighted mean. Comparisons with other people use a 30% standard deviation, the simulated spread between people. Values: set date = 3 x spread / cost; weeks to save $50 = 50 x cost / wait; face to face = video / cost.

    Attention and choices

    In the simulated laboratory data, each standard deviation more attention to waiting information changed the weight on uncertainty by {{ATTN_EFFECT}}. This is a hypothesis the study will test. The tool does not use it: attention never changes a person's weights, best match or ranking. Attention is used only to show people where they looked, to ask them to check the latest week if they barely looked at it before choosing, and to remind them in their plan about things they hardly looked at.

    How the camera is used

    MediaPipe Face Landmarker (Apache 2.0) runs in the browser and finds the eyes, irises and head pose in each video frame (CPU on phones and tablets, GPU then CPU on computers). Features: each iris position within its eye (across and up-down), nose position and head turn. Frames during blinks are dropped. Features are standardised and a 9-point calibration fits a ridge regression to screen position, then 4 automatic dots measure accuracy as a share of the screen. During the 6 choices the comparison fills the screen, so each area (when, weeks, cost, how) is large. Smoothed gaze points are matched to the area under them about 20 to 30 times a second. Webcam eye tracking can reach about 1.4 degrees of accuracy in good conditions (Kaduk et al. 2023); simpler set-ups are less accurate (Papoutsaki et al. 2016). Video frames are never stored. On computers without the camera, pointer position is used in the style of mouse-tracking studies.

    Waiting times

    A range a person enters (for example 3 to 9 weeks) is treated as an equal chance in each week. Clinic records use a Kaplan-Meier estimate: waits of people seen are events and people still waiting are censored at the time they have waited so far, so the longest waits are not dropped. The chance of being seen in the next 2 weeks for someone who has waited w weeks is (S(w) - S(w + 2)) / S(w). The choice study cannot produce these chances, because choices between hypothetical appointments say nothing about a clinic's capacity.

    Not yet validated. Before these chances can be called forecasts, they must be checked against real records: weekly snapshots of who is on each waiting list and since when, appointment dates, cancellations, people who leave the list, and changes in clinician hours. At each snapshot the method predicts each person's chance of a first appointment in the next 2 weeks; this is then compared with what happened, by calibration (predicted against observed, in bands) and Brier score, with regular refitting. The engine includes this check (validateGuide). Until then the tool calls these numbers a rough guide.

    Costs

    Medicare benefits from 1 July 2026 for sessions of 50 minutes or more: psychologist $101.55 (MBS item 80110, schedule fee $119.45), clinical psychologist $149.05, mental health social worker or occupational therapist $89.50. Up to 10 individual sessions a calendar year with a Mental Health Treatment Plan and a referral from the person's MyMedicare practice or usual GP; the first referral covers up to 6, then a GP review. Video and phone sessions are covered with the same limits; the same benefit is assumed and labelled as such. Each session is paid one way: Medicare, or private health extras, or in full. Extras never top up a Medicare session. A benefit is never more than the fee.
