    How the research shapes this tool

    Know Your Wait is built on a University of Newcastle study. Each part of the study changes something you see.

    The study has not started. All study numbers on this site are simulated to show how the tool will work.

    Online choice study

    200 adults choose between appointments

    Each person makes 8 choices between appointments with different waits, costs and certainty.

    Gives the starting point for your trade-offs

    40 people in our lab

    How your answers are used

    We start from the study's average weights for waiting time, uncertainty, cost and video, and allow each weight to vary by 50% between people. We draw 4,000 possible sets of weights, score how well each set explains your 6 choices with a logit model, and take the weighted average. Your comparison with others uses the spread between people in the study (30%).

    Real evidence behind the tool

    • 1 in 4 people who needed a psychologist delayed or did not go because of cost (ABS 2025).
    • Under Better Access, people on the lowest incomes waited longer for a first session than people on the highest incomes (Pirkis et al. 2022).
    • People who wait longer are less likely to attend their first appointment (Loumidis and Shropshire 1997).
    • Support while waiting is seen as useful, but people stay with it better when a person guides them (Huang et al. 2024).
    • Showing chances as "X in 10" with simple pictures helps people with low numeracy (Galesic et al. 2009).
