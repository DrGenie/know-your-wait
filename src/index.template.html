About this prototype

Know Your Wait is a research prototype from the University of Newcastle pilot study Waiting, cost and certainty: preferences for access to psychological care. It shows how evidence from a discrete choice experiment and an eye-tracking study could help people compare appointment offers, and help services describe uncertain waiting times.

How it works

  • Each option is turned into a week-by-week chance of being seen. A confirmed date puts all of the chance on one week. A range spreads it equally over the weeks in the range.
  • From that distribution the tool reports the chance of being seen by your chosen week, the earliest and latest week, the average wait and how much the wait could vary.
  • The fit scores use a conditional logit model. Each option's score combines its average wait, how much the wait varies, its cost per session and whether it is by video, weighted by the pilot's estimates and by your own sliders.
  • The clinic page summarises recent waits with simple percentiles and presents them as natural frequencies, such as "8 in 10 people", which are easier to understand than percentages.

Limits

  • The pilot results are simulated until the study is complete.
  • Fit scores show how well an option matches the priorities you set. They are not clinical advice. Talk to your GP about which care is right for you.
  • The pilot tested expected waits of 4 to 8 weeks, ranges of up to 3 weeks either side and costs of $0 to $120 per session. Options outside these ranges are flagged.
  • The prototype was developed with reference to the International Patient Decision Aid Standards. It has not been evaluated against them.
